Nigeria's visa system can look intimidating from the outside. Multiple categories, evolving online processes, varying lead times — it is easy to choose the wrong visa, and the consequences range from minor inconvenience to a refused entry at the airport.

This article breaks down the main visa categories used by foreign nationals entering Nigeria, who each one is for, and how to choose correctly.

## Why Choosing the Right Visa Matters

A visa is not just a stamp — it defines what an individual may lawfully do in Nigeria. The wrong visa can:

- Result in refusal of entry at the port of entry
- Limit the activities the holder can undertake
- Create complications when applying for residence permits later
- Trigger penalties for both the traveller and the employing company

Selecting the right category from the outset is by far the easiest way to avoid these issues.

## The Main Visa Categories

### Business Visa

The **Business Visa** is for foreign nationals visiting Nigeria for business activities that **do not constitute employment**. Typical use cases include:

- Business meetings and conferences
- Contract negotiations
- Site inspections and audits
- Supplier or distributor visits

It is not a work permit, and it should not be used for hands-on project work or anything that resembles employment in Nigeria.

### Temporary Work Permit (TWP)

The **TWP** is for foreign nationals who need to carry out **short-term work assignments** in Nigeria without holding an expatriate quota position. Typical use cases include:

- Equipment installations and commissioning
- Technical support and field service
- Audits, training, and knowledge-transfer assignments
- Specialist consultants on time-bound projects

The TWP requires pre-approval from the NIS, and is usually issued for a limited period (commonly 90 days, with possible extensions).

### Subject to Regularisation (STR) Visa

The **STR** visa is the long-term work visa category. It is used when an expatriate is taking up a position tied to the employing company's Expatriate Quota. STR visa holders are required to **regularise their stay** in Nigeria by obtaining a CERPAC within the prescribed timeframe.

This is the visa most commonly used for permanent or multi-year expatriate roles.

### Tourist Visa

The **Tourist Visa** is for non-business visits — leisure, family visits, or other personal travel. It is straightforward but, like all categories, must match the actual purpose of the trip.

### Visa on Arrival (VoA)

The **Visa on Arrival** scheme allows pre-approved travellers to receive their visa stamp at the Nigerian port of entry. There are two relevant variants:

- **VoA — Business** — For high-priority business travellers needing expedited processing
- **VoA — TWP** — For short-term work assignments where the TWP is issued on arrival

VoA always requires pre-approval before travel — the actual visa is issued at the airport, but the approval must already be in place.

## How to Choose

A simple decision framework:

| Purpose of visit | Recommended visa |
| --- | --- |
| Meetings, negotiations, site visits | Business Visa or VoA — Business |
| Short-term technical work, installations, training | TWP or VoA — TWP |
| Long-term employment in Nigeria | STR Visa (followed by CERPAC) |
| Leisure, family visit | Tourist Visa |

When in doubt — and especially when multiple categories could arguably apply — the safe approach is to err on the side of a permit that authorises more, not less. A business visa used for hands-on technical work is a recipe for trouble at the airport.

## Documentation: The Underrated Factor

The visa category is only half the story. The other half is documentation. A well-prepared application — with the right invitation letter, supporting company documents, financial evidence, and travel details — moves through faster and avoids requests for additional information.

This is where many self-prepared applications fail. The category may be correct, but the package is incomplete.
