## Why Choosing the Right Visa Matters

A visa is not just a stamp — it defines what an individual may lawfully do in Nigeria. The wrong visa can:

- Result in refusal of entry at the port of entry
- Result in reduced duration of stay or total denial of entry
- Limit the activities the holder can undertake
- Create complications or blacklisting that negatively affects renewal or subsequent visa applications
- Trigger penalties for both the traveller and the employing company or host

Selecting the right category from the outset is by far the easiest way to avoid these issues.

## The Main Visa Categories

### Business Visa

The **Business Visa** is for foreign nationals visiting Nigeria for business activities that **do not constitute employment**. Typical use cases include:

- Business meetings and conferences
- Contract negotiations
- Site inspections and audits
- Supplier or distributor visits
- Interviews, trade fairs, exhibitions

It is not a work permit, and it should not be used for hands-on project work or anything that resembles employment in Nigeria.

### Temporary Work Permit (TWP)

The **TWP** is for foreign nationals who need to carry out **short-term work assignments** in Nigeria without holding an expatriate quota position. Typical use cases include:

- Equipment installations and commissioning
- Technical support and field service
- Audits, training, and knowledge-transfer assignments
- Specialist consultants on time-bound projects

The TWP requires pre-approval from the NIS, and is usually issued for a limited period (commonly 90 days, without possible extensions).

### Subject to Regularisation (STR) Visa

The **STR** visa is the long-term work visa category. It is used when an expatriate is taking up a position tied to the employing company's Expatriate Quota. STR visa holders are required to **regularise their stay** in Nigeria by obtaining a CERPAC within the prescribed timeframe. Under the new visa regulations, STR visa holders will be issued an e-CERPAC embedded in machine-readable QR codes prior to arrival in Nigeria.

This is the visa most commonly used for permanent or multi-year expatriate roles.
