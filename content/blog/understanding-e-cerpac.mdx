The Combined Expatriate Residence Permit and Aliens Card — commonly known as E-CERPAC or the Nigerian Green Card — is the single most important residency document for foreign nationals living and working in Nigeria. It is required by law, biometrically issued, and tied directly to an expatriate's lawful right to reside in the country.

For HR managers and mobility leads, understanding E-CERPAC is not optional. Here is what every employer of foreign nationals (expatriates) in Nigeria needs to know.

## What is the E-CERPAC?

The E-CERPAC is a biometric residence permit issued by the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS). It serves as:

- **Proof of lawful residence** in Nigeria
- **Authorisation to live and work** under the conditions of an associated Expatriate Quota position
- **A key identifier** for re-entry, banking, schooling, and other day-to-day activities

It is mandatory for all foreign nationals living in Nigeria. Operating without one — or on an expired card — exposes both the employee and the employing organisation to serious risk.

## The E-CERPAC Lifecycle

The E-CERPAC is not a one-off document. It is part of an ongoing lifecycle that begins upon issuance — whether the expatriate is within or outside Nigeria — and continues until the expiry date clearly shown on the card.

Previously, expatriates entered Nigeria on a Subject to Regularisation (STR) visa and were required to regularise their stay within a specified timeframe — typically within 90 days of arrival. **Effective January 2026, expatriates coming to Nigeria on a new work/STR visa are required to visit the nearest Nigerian Embassy or High Commission for biometric capture, and their e-CERPAC will be processed and issued electronically before they proceed to Nigeria.**

Expatriates who are currently in Nigeria while their CERPACs have expired are required to process their e-CERPACs online and thereafter schedule an appointment at the nearest Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS) command for their biometrics. An e-CERPAC is then processed and issued electronically. One notable feature of the current process is the total lack of physical contact with the travel passport of the expatriate.

### 1. Summary of the Process

The E-CERPAC is issued for a period of one year. The process involves:

- Submission of an application via the NIS portal
- Biometric capture (fingerprints and photograph)
- Document verification
- Card production and issuance

### 2. Renewal

E-CERPAC cards must be renewed before expiry. There are two scenarios:

- **In-Country Renewal** — For expatriates currently in Nigeria. The renewal is initiated well in advance of the expiry date.
- **Out-Country Renewal** — For expatriates who happen to be outside Nigeria as their card approaches expiry. The renewal is processed remotely and the biometric process is conducted at a Nigerian Embassy or High Commission outside Nigeria.

### 3. Migration / Transitioning

If an expatriate's immigration status changes — for example, moving from a Temporary Work Permit to an STR position, or transferring between employers (a "Change of Employment") — the E-CERPAC must be updated. This is often referred to as migration or transitioning, and it ensures the card always reflects the holder's current status.

### 4. Landing Page (Entry and Exit)

Every time an expatriate arrives in or departs from Nigeria, an electronic Landing Page must be completed. This is a small but important compliance item, and missing entries can complicate future applications.

## Common E-CERPAC Pitfalls

Across many years of supporting expatriates in Nigeria, the most frequent E-CERPAC issues we see are:

- **Missed renewals** — Resulting in fines and gaps in lawful residence
- **Outdated information** — An E-CERPAC that does not reflect a change in employer or role
- **Forgotten landing pages** — Especially during short business trips within or out of country
- **Lost or damaged cards (historic)** — Replacement procedures used to take time and require documentation. With the new e-CERPAC, physical cards have become obsolete: the QR code contains all relevant data and can be stored on phones and other devices.

## Why Proactive E-CERPAC Management Matters

Unlike a passport, the E-CERPAC is not a document that sits quietly in a drawer. It is connected to the expatriate's right to work, to bank, to travel, and — in some cases — to remain in the country. Lapses are not theoretical risks; they are operational disruptions.

Companies that manage E-CERPAC on an ad-hoc basis often face avoidable issues. Companies that manage it proactively — with monitoring systems, advance renewal triggers, and clear documentation — avoid them entirely.
