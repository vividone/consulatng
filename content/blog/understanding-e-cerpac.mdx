The Combined Expatriate Residence Permit and Aliens Card — universally known as CERPAC — is the single most important residency document for foreign nationals living and working in Nigeria. It is required by law, biometrically issued, and tied directly to an expatriate's lawful right to reside in the country.

For HR managers and mobility leads, understanding CERPAC is not optional. Here is what every employer of foreign nationals in Nigeria needs to know.

## What is the CERPAC?

The CERPAC is a biometric residence permit issued by the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS). It serves as:

- **Proof of lawful residence** in Nigeria
- **Authorisation to live and work** under the conditions of an associated quota position
- **A key identifier** for re-entry, banking, schooling, and other day-to-day activities

It is mandatory for all foreign nationals living in Nigeria beyond a defined initial period. Operating without one — or on an expired card — exposes both the employee and the employing organisation to serious risk.

## The CERPAC Lifecycle

The CERPAC is not a one-off document. It is part of an ongoing lifecycle that begins on arrival and continues for as long as the expatriate is in country.

### 1. Regularisation

Expatriates who enter Nigeria on a Subject to Regularisation (STR) visa must regularise their stay by obtaining a CERPAC within a specified timeframe — typically within 90 days of arrival. Missing this window can result in penalties, overstay flags, and complications with future renewals.

### 2. First-Time Issuance

After regularisation, the CERPAC is issued for an initial period (commonly one or two years). The process involves:

- Submission of an application via the NIS portal
- Biometric capture (fingerprints and photograph)
- Document verification
- Card production and issuance

### 3. Renewal

CERPAC cards must be renewed before expiry. There are two scenarios:

- **In-Country Renewal** — For expatriates currently in Nigeria. The renewal is initiated well in advance of the expiry date.
- **Out-Country Renewal** — For expatriates who happen to be outside Nigeria as their card approaches expiry. The process is coordinated remotely so the renewed card is ready upon return.

### 4. Migration / Transitioning

If an expatriate's immigration status changes — for example, moving from a Temporary Work Permit to an STR position, or transferring between employers — the CERPAC must be updated. This is often referred to as migration or transitioning, and it ensures the card always reflects the holder's current status.

### 5. Landing Page (Entry and Exit)

Every time an expatriate arrives in or departs from Nigeria, an electronic Landing Page must be completed. This is a small but important compliance item, and missing entries can complicate future applications.

## Common CERPAC Pitfalls

Across many years of supporting expatriates in Nigeria, the most frequent CERPAC issues we see are:

- **Late regularisation** — Triggering overstay flags that follow the expatriate for years
- **Missed renewals** — Resulting in fines and gaps in lawful residence
- **Outdated information** — A CERPAC that does not reflect a change in employer or role
- **Forgotten landing pages** — Especially during short business trips out of country
- **Lost or damaged cards** — Replacement procedures take time and require documentation

Each of these can be avoided with proper monitoring and proactive management.

## Why Proactive CERPAC Management Matters

Unlike a passport, the CERPAC is not a document that sits quietly in a drawer. It is connected to the expatriate's right to work, to bank, to travel, and — in some cases — to remain in the country. Lapses are not theoretical risks; they are operational disruptions.

Companies that manage CERPAC on an ad-hoc basis often face avoidable issues. Companies that manage it proactively — with monitoring systems, advance renewal triggers, and clear documentation — avoid them entirely.
